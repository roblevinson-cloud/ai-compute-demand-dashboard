Investment conclusion

The cited November 18 refresh improves destination coverage; it does not revise national volume or value and does not change the 2026 national run-rate. October CPCA volume, newly fetched for this update, is 928,000 vehicles. Momentum remains strong year over year, with a much slower sequential pace. The data support continued overseas competitive pressure, but do not establish a 2027–2030 growth rate or Chinese-brand market share.

October’s original CPCA release reports +68.5% YoY. Our saved original monthly observations yield +63.4% October YoY (928k / 568k − 1). The discrepancy suggests different data vintages or definitions; the exact cause has not been reconciled. We do not infer revised historical months from a reported growth rate. We retain original monthly levels and distinguish release growth from recomputed growth. The release also reports 810k own-brand and 118k joint-venture/luxury exports in October, illustrating why China-origin exports are broader than Chinese-brand sales.

Quarterly volume rose from 1.826m in Q1 to 2.430m in Q2 and 2.723m in Q3. September increased 3.3% MoM; October increased 1.2% from the saved September level. This is sequential moderation at a high level, not evidence of export contraction. A November–December pace of 0.8–1.0m per month gives roughly 9.51–9.91m for 2026. These calculations include CKD kits and are not finished-vehicle shipping capacity requirements.

October customs value and destination observations remain blank until reported. National value per vehicle is not calculated by dividing HS 8703 value by CPCA units because the populations differ.

What changes for 2027–2030

The November 18 revisions alone warrant no change to a national export forecast. They strengthen the evidence for specific reported destinations, but do not prove broad European penetration or invalidate a plateau risk. October’s volume confirms a high exit pace; it provides no basis to extend 2026’s roughly 75% year-to-date growth for another four years.

For competitive analysis, use Chinese OEM destination retail sales = China-origin deliveries sold locally + locally manufactured vehicles sold locally, with inventory and re-exports reconciled. CKD kits counted in exports can become locally assembled vehicles: adding the two series without reconciling kits double counts demand. Production abroad and exports are alternative supply routes, so export flattening can coexist with further Chinese OEM share gains.

The practical Stellantis question is how many additional Chinese OEM retail sales overlap its B/C cars and crossovers, at what transaction prices, in each European market. Export growth alone cannot be assigned one-for-one to Stellantis lost production. Total-market growth, other incumbent losses, inventories and Stellantis’s own launches must be included.
