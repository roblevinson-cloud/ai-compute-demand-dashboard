Quarterly import signal: useful scale check, weak timing signal

Q1 2024–Q2 2026: estimated consumption exceeds Petrobras domestic naphtha sales in every quarter. The residual totals 8.81Mt, compared with 8.43Mt of national imports (+4.6%). However, quarterly level correlation is 0.49, mean absolute error is 130kt / 17.1%, and only 3 of 9 quarter-to-quarter directions agree. Change correlation is 0.03.

In Q1→Q2 2026, estimated imports rise 8.2%, while reported national imports and Braskem customs both fall 44.4%. This does not validate the residual as a reliable quarterly import indicator.

Calculation, assumptions and sources

Consumption = Brazil fossil ethylene × (1 − estimated non-naphtha share) / 0.33. Braskem's latest valuation book reports quarterly total Brazil ethylene, but does not provide quarterly Brazilian gas ethylene separately. Mexico ethane output and green operations are separate and excluded. The base allocation assumes the 650kt/y structural gas capacity and all other Brazil units share utilization, giving a gas share of 650/3,752 = 17.3%.

The alternate case adds maximum Bahia gas flexibility: 15% × 1,280kt/y, for 22.4% non-naphtha ethylene. This changes the aggregate residual to 7.80Mt (−7.5% vs national imports), without improving quarterly correlation (0.48). Latest capacities are assumed applicable to the whole history; actual gas utilization could differ.

Petrobras tonnes = reported thousand barrels/day × 1,000 × actual quarter days × 0.158987294928m³/barrel × 0.73t/m³. For example, Q2 2026: 86 × 1,000 × 91 × 0.158987294928 × 0.73 = 908,291 tonnes. National ANP m³ use the same density; Braskem customs use actual kg for NCM 27101241.

Consumption minus Petrobras sales is an import requirement estimate. Inventory changes, other domestic suppliers and customers, feedstock mix, yields and customs clearance timing prevent it from being an identity. Petrobras sales are domestic sales to all customers, not a disclosed Braskem-only volume. No fitted adjustment or lag was used.
